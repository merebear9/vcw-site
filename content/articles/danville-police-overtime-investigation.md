Overtime payments to Danville Police Department officers nearly tripled between 2023 and 2025, according to payroll records obtained by Vermilion County Watchdog through a six-month records request process — and internal documents show the department's own overtime approval policy went largely unenforced during that period.

This is the first installment in a three-part investigative series examining overtime spending, staffing shortages, and accountability gaps inside the Danville Police Department.

## The Numbers

Payroll data obtained through a Freedom of Information Act request shows total departmental overtime spending rose from roughly $410,000 in fiscal year 2023 to more than $1.1 million in fiscal year 2025 — a 168% increase — even as the department's authorized staffing level remained essentially flat.

A small cluster of officers accounted for a disproportionate share of the increase. Five individuals collected more than $80,000 each in overtime pay in FY2025 alone, on top of their base salaries.

## No One Was Watching

Internal policy obtained by this newsroom requires the deputy chief to review and sign off on any individual officer's overtime hours exceeding 20 hours in a pay period. Vermilion County Watchdog cross-referenced 26 pay periods against sign-off logs and found documented approvals for only 4 of them.

"The policy exists on paper," said a source familiar with the department's payroll process who was not authorized to speak publicly. "Whether anyone was actually checking it is a different question."

## What We're Still Investigating

Part two of this series, publishing next week for members, will examine specific incidents that triggered unusually large overtime payouts, including a single week in which one officer logged 94 hours.

Part three will look at what comparable departments in downstate Illinois pay in overtime and whether Danville's oversight gap is unusual.
